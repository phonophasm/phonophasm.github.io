I’ve started my PhD at the Sonic Arts Research Centre (SARC) in Belfast. After ten years abroad, I’m getting used to university life here again, as well as the less structured task of creating my own routine. Moving across the world and beginning a research project at the same time has been exciting, informative and, at moments, a little overwhelming.

A good part of these first weeks has been meeting my supervisors, other students and researchers at SARC. I’ve found people to play music with, ask questions and bounce ideas around with. Those conversations have already made the move feel less like starting from scratch.

My PhD project, **Re-Sounding Identities**, is still at an early stage. I’m exploring how people seeking asylum might use VR music-making and storytelling to explore identity and belonging on their own terms. I’m interested in what becomes possible when someone can make and shape sound in an immersive space, and in the choices they should have over what, if anything, they share with others. The participant work has not begun; for now, I’m learning, building relationships and thinking carefully about how to approach it.

Alongside that, I’ve begun testing [PHONOSPHERE]({{ '/projects/phonosphere/' | relative_url }}) in the surround studio at SARC. Hearing the instrument through a room of speakers is quite different from hearing it through headphones. I’ve run into a few issues with the setup that need more work, but the early tests are encouraging: the interaction between movement in VR and sound in the room seems capable of working the way I’ve imagined.

I’ve also enjoyed trying PHONOSPHERE with QUBe, a group of musicians making improvised experimental music. Playing alongside them is a useful way to discover what the instrument does in a shared musical setting, and it’s been a pleasure to play with such inventive people. I am planning to join QUBe for [HandMade Music in Belfast on 5 November](https://soundofbelfast.com/events/051126-5) which will be the first time I play live here in Northern Ireland and I'm looking forward to it, as well as meeting other creatives and seeing some local talent.

There is plenty to work through, technically and personally, but I’m looking forward to the people and challenges ahead. I’ll share more as the research develops, the experiments continue and I find my rhythm in Belfast.
